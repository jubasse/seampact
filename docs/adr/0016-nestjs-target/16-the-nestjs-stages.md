### The NestJS stages

Steps 6 to 8 run in a seampact pipe on the operation's input param, in every mode, and the target option `reply: interceptor` lets a seampact interceptor run step 10 and write the reply. NestJS's roles on a contract route are then: the guard checks access, the pipe validates, the wrapper or the reply interceptor replies. There is no seampact interceptor otherwise: on any route with an interceptor, NestJS 12 runs the handler through a per-request path of its own (two `AsyncResource.bind` calls, an rxjs `defer` chain, a client-disconnect watcher) that costs about 0.6 of a baseline request, and on 12.1.x each bound resource's native memory outlives the GC (off-heap growth past 150 MB over 40,000 requests, against about 40 MB without); NestJS 12.2.0 cures the growth, not the CPU cost.

NestJS's stages on a contract route, in request order (NestJS 12.2.0, nestjs/nest#18171 still open):

| NestJS stage | handler mode (default) | interceptor mode |
|---|---|---|
| app-wide parser, app middleware | step 1 parser refusals (pre-routing filter) | same |
| seampact module middleware | step 1 contract part (body limit, media type), request context, outgoing check | same |
| global guards (`useGlobalGuards`, `APP_GUARD`), then controller guards | the app's | same |
| route guards | every app method guard, wherever written, then the seampact guard last: steps 2 to 5 | same |
| interceptors, pre | the app's, before steps 6 to 8 | the reply interceptor, then the app's |
| pipes | the app's; on the input param the seampact pipe last: steps 6 to 8 | same |
| handler | wrapper: replay or claim, the method, step 10, the write | wrapper: replay (written) or claim, the method, its result returned |
| interceptors, post | the app's see `undefined` | the app's map the result; the reply interceptor runs step 10 on it and writes; an app global interceptor another module's `configure` puts before it sees `undefined` (warned at boot) |
| exception filters (route, controller, global) | the seampact filter for contract errors and mapped refusals, else the app's; outgoing check on every reply | same |

NestJS appends a method's guards in the order their decorators apply, bottom-up, so an app guard written above the composite would come after the seampact guard. The module's `configure`, which NestJS calls before it reads any route's guards, moves the seampact guard to the end of the guard list of each controller method that carries one of the api's operations: the composite's wrapper, or the function a decorator written above it put in its place with the metadata copied (a tracing `@Span()`).

What an app observes:

- Its guards: global and controller guards run first; a method guard runs before the seampact guard whichever side of the composite it is written. A passport 401 or 403 is mapped to the operation's role by the seampact filter ("Exceptions on contract routes").
- Its pipes: they run before steps 6 to 8 on every param and see `undefined` on the input param, their result ignored; a global `ValidationPipe` skips the input param (a custom param), and with `validateCustomDecorators` returns it untouched when the param's design type is `Object` or absent.
- Its interceptors: their pre-phase sees the request before validation, and a refusal of step 6, 7 or 8 passes through their `catchError`. One that rethrows another exception hands the reply to the app's filters, and the outgoing check holds the contract: a 500 under `strict`, a logged violation under `warn`. In the handler mode their post-phase sees `undefined` once the reply is written, so a response-transforming interceptor (`ClassSerializerInterceptor`, a `map`) changes nothing on the wire; a result mapping belongs in the handler, so the reply written is the one checked against the contract.
- An interceptor that skips the handler, in the default reply mode: the module's `configure` places the reply interceptor outside the app's interceptors of each route that has one, in front of the global list when the app has a global interceptor, else first on the controller or the method that binds one. Its post-phase sees `undefined` once the wrapper wrote the reply; a value it receives with no reply written is checked and written as in the interceptor mode, and one that skipped the input pipe answers 500 with a log (`isContractRoute` stays the way to skip). Routes without an app interceptor stay off NestJS's interceptor path. A global interceptor another module's `configure` adds after this one is not covered.
- In the interceptor mode, the reply interceptor is an `APP_INTERCEPTOR` of the api's module. NestJS orders `APP_INTERCEPTOR` providers by module scanning, the app module's first, so the module's `configure` moves the reply interceptor to the front of `ApplicationConfig`'s global list, in place since the class has no setter; NestJS reads that list when it registers the routes, after every `configure`. It then sits outside every other global interceptor, `APP_INTERCEPTOR` or `useGlobalInterceptors`, every controller and route interceptor and every request-scoped global one, and their mappings are the value step 10 checks. The boot names a global another module's `configure` puts in front of it afterwards. On a replay the wrapper writes the stored reply and the app's interceptors see `undefined`; an app interceptor that fails after the claim is answered as a handler exception, the record completed with that reply or abandoned on a 500. The response stays marked with `Res()`, so NestJS writes nothing after the boundary.
- An idempotent replay is made in the wrapper, so the app's pipes and route interceptors run before the stored reply is replayed.
- Its filters: the seampact filter catches the generated contract exceptions and the mapped refusals, thrown from the guard, the pipe or the wrapper, and every reply passes the outgoing check.
- The boot: the global-interceptor warnings, the cache interceptor refusal, the guard-last check and the input-param check of "The boot check".

The pipe costs one awaited pipe chain NestJS gives any param with a pipe (nestjs/nest#18147), measured on the bench's nestjs-zod pair on NestJS 12.2.0 in the handler mode (throughput over the hand-written baseline, four runs each): health, a route with no work, went from about 0.785 to about 0.74; listTasks stayed at about 0.62. It is accepted: it shows only on a no-op route, and validation in a pipe is NestJS's idiom. The interceptor mode puts every route on NestJS's interceptor path: health about 0.61 without an app interceptor, 0.70 with one global app interceptor (0.79 in the handler mode).

What the boundary guarantees: steps 3 to 8 run after every app guard and after step 2; steps 6 to 8 complete before the handler, the handler receives only their output, and neither the method nor step 10 runs without them (fail closed, a 500); the reply is written once, by the boundary, after step 10, from the handler's result (handler mode) or from the value the app's interceptors inside the reply interceptor pass on (interceptor mode); no app pipe can refuse after an idempotency claim, since every param's pipes resolve before the wrapper claims; the outgoing check sees every reply on a contract route.
